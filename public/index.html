01 Non-profit partner · Matmissionen Make sense of messy documents, and know when to trust them.

Matmissionen is part of Stockholms Stadsmission. It runs social supermarkets and moves donated food across Sweden. Running that operation creates a steady stream of documents: transport invoices from many carriers, and other running costs such as energy bills. Today someone reads each one by hand and types the key numbers into Excel.

The problem today

  • Every company sends its documents in its own layout. Some are neat tables, others are “all over the place”.
  • Not every document has every field, and some contain errors. When data is missing or wrong, it is easy not to notice.
  • The numbers feed statistics that Matmissionen relies on: pallets sent, weight, cost per pallet and per kilogram, and transports per city.
  • When the work piles up for a few weeks, catching up is slow and mistakes slip in.
  • Transport invoices are only the start. The same work applies to energy bills and other running costs.

What to build

A workflow that turns documents in any layout into data Matmissionen can trust. Getting the numbers out is only half of it. The workflow must also check what it reads, say clearly when a document is incomplete or does not add up, and explain why.

A strong solution will:

  1. Read documents in different layouts and pull out the same fields from each one.
  2. Check the data for missing fields, totals that do not add up, odd values and duplicates.
  3. Flag problems in plain words, naming the document and the field, instead of guessing.
  4. Keep the statistics up to date and show the totals Matmissionen needs.
  5. Make it easy to add a new kind of document, such as an energy bill, without starting over.

Stretch goals

A data quality view per supplier, showing who often leaves out fields or makes errors. A simple way for a person to check and correct flagged items. Plain-language insights on how costs change over time.

What you get

Sample invoices from several carriers and an example of the Excel sheet, all anonymised or made up. Matmissionen explains the challenge in a short video at kickoff.

02 Non-profit partner · Entrepreneurs Without Borders Help good ideas travel.

Entrepreneurs Without Borders (EWB) is an international non-profit that uses entrepreneurship to drive social, economic and environmental change. It is building a global network of local Impact Chapters, and wants good solutions to travel between them.

The problem today

All over the world, people build solutions to problems that others are still struggling with. But those solutions often stay where they started, and people elsewhere keep reinventing the wheel. A solution that works in one place may also fail in another, because of differences in money, culture, climate, rules or local needs.

At the same time, many people want to do something for their community but don’t know what is realistic or where to start.

A real example: SOSO Care

SOSO Care, a Nigerian social enterprise that EWB supported, lets people pay for health insurance with recyclable waste. When EWB looked at bringing it to Rwanda, they hit a problem: most people in Rwanda already have community health insurance, so the main reason to join was gone. Could the model work with a different reward? What local facts decide whether it succeeds? And who in Rwanda could make it happen?

What to build

The question for your team: how might we help people anywhere find solutions that are proven somewhere else, understand whether they could work locally, and take the first steps to make them happen?

The approach is open on purpose. EWB doesn’t expect a full platform. Pick one important part of the journey and build a working prototype of it, for example:

Find

Match a person’s skills, time and local problem with proven solutions from elsewhere.

Check fit

Compare a solution’s original setting with a new place and show what would need to change.

Connect

Point people to collaborators, knowledge and resources nearby.

Act

Turn good intentions into a clear first step and a short plan.

Two users to design for: someone in a Houston suburb with logistics experience who wants to create local jobs, and a Nigerian entrepreneur who wants to grow their model into new countries.

What you get

The SOSO Care example and sample impact business models, all as made-up or public data. Someone from EWB presents the challenge at kickoff.

03 Special challenge · Stockholm AI Help a volunteer-run community run itself.

Stockholm AI is a non-profit tech community run by volunteers. Behind every meetup, hackathon and newsletter there is a lot of manual work: screening sign-ups, matching people into teams, chasing no-shows, gathering news for the weekly newsletter and keeping track of partners.

What to build

Pick one piece of community work that volunteers still do by hand, and automate it end to end. For example:

Screen

Check event sign-ups and suggest who to approve, waitlist or follow up with.

Match

Build balanced teams from sign-up answers, skills and preferences.

Curate

Gather and summarise AI news from Stockholm into a newsletter draft.

Follow up

Remind, confirm and thank attendees, and free up spots for the waitlist.

The solution should work for any volunteer-run community, not only Stockholm AI.

What you get

Made-up sign-up lists and a sample newsletter issue. The Stockholm AI board picks the winner.

04 Optional track · Bring your own non-profit problem Know a non-profit that needs help? Bring its problem.

Instead of the challenges above, a team can work on a real problem from a non-profit, charity or community group they know.

To take part

  1. Post in #hack4good-participants before the online kickoff on Wednesday 2 December: the organisation, the problem in two or three sentences, and who would use the solution.
  2. Make sure the organisation knows about it and is happy for you to work on it.
  3. Use made-up data only, as in every challenge.
  4. In your pitch, show how the organisation could start using it.

Judging

The organisers and mentors pick the winner, using the same scorecard as the other challenges.

04 / Rules for all teams

Six ground rules.

  1. 01

    Made-up data only

    Use the sample data we give you. Never upload real personal data or real invoices from anyone.

  2. 02

    Build in n8n

    Every team gets n8n Cloud access on the day. You can call any AI model or outside tool from your workflow.

  3. 03

    Show it working

    A small workflow that runs end to end beats a big idea on slides.

  4. 04

    Freeze at 14:30

    Stop building, then post your team name, challenge and the link to your n8n workflow in Slack.

  5. 05

    Ask for help early

    Mentors and the organisers are around all day. Find someone in an n8n Stockholm or Stockholm AI shirt.

  6. 06

    Hand it over

    After the event, we share the winning workflows and notes with the partners, so they can keep using them if they want to.
